The whole point of mitigation is to stop the damage from growing. It is not the repair. It is everything done before the repair to protect what is still sound. In Muskegon, our team handles mitigation by controlling the source, removing the water, containing the wet area and stabilizing the structure. Good mitigation makes the later repair smaller, and now and then makes it unnecessary.

Many homeowners treat restoration and mitigation as one and the same. In fact there are two separate stages. During mitigation, the urgent part, the loss is halted, its spread is limited and the home is made stable. Restoration is what comes after: drying is confirmed, and damaged drywall, flooring and finishes are repaired or rebuilt. Insurance companies also bill the two as separate line items, which makes it useful to know your current phase.

A typical mitigation visit covers four things. First is source control, which keeps more water from coming in. Second is extraction, to clear out standing water. Three: contain the area with plastic sheeting and managed airflow, so clean rooms stay free of moisture and contaminants. Stabilization, which means removing materials that cannot be saved, treating surfaces with antimicrobial, and setting air movers and dehumidifiers. We also protect furniture and belongings by moving or blocking them up.

Why Water Damage Mitigation Is Not a DIY Job

Doing mitigation well means making a string of quick judgment calls. Can this section of drywall dry in place, or does it need cutting out? Which category applies, 1, 2 or 3, and did that change after the leak first began? Should the toe kick under the cabinets be pulled? We answer those questions with training and moisture readings rather than by the look or feel of a surface.

Good mitigation also protects your claim. Owners are required by most policies to take reasonable steps against further damage, and the insurer will ask for evidence. On arrival we take photos, then log moisture readings every day and itemize whatever is removed. Together those records show the water's effect and our response to it. Whoever handles repairs then begins from a known condition, and nothing wet ends up sealed behind new wallboard.

Signs It Is Time for Water Damage Mitigation

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • The insurance company instructed you to take reasonable action against further damage.
  • Following a water loss, indoor air stays damp and condensation forms on the glass.
  • Carpet squishes underfoot, or the pad beneath it is saturated.
  • A plumber fixed the pipe but nobody has dealt with the water.
  • Since the leak you have noticed puffed-up baseboards or gaps opening behind them.
  • The space under your cabinets in the bathroom or kitchen took on water you cannot get to.
  • Flooring is carrying moisture past the doorway to an adjoining room.

Water Damage Mitigation Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Slope the soil away from the house so rainwater does not pool against the walls.
  • A monthly look around toilets and inside sink cabinets catches drips, staining and spongy flooring.
  • Have your water heater inspected yearly and replace it before the tank fails.
  • Replace rubber washing machine hoses with braided steel lines every five years or so.
  • A main shutoff that will not turn is no help, so locate and test yours.
  • Seal gaps around windows, doors and roof penetrations before the next heavy rain arrives.
