Kitchen Water Damage in Muskegon

Count the dishwasher, disposal, drain, faucet and fridge line, and the kitchen has more places for water to connect than anywhere else in the house. Most of these connections are tucked into cabinets or hidden at the back of appliances. If one starts leaking, water heads for the back of the base cabinet and works its way under the floor. Our Muskegon crew locates the leak, dries what it wet, and keeps as much of your kitchen intact as we can.

The costly piece of a kitchen leak is the cabinets. Plywood and solid wood will often dry successfully, whereas a particleboard box puffs up quickly and never returns to size. By removing toe kicks and drilling small holes where they will not show, we push dry air beneath and behind the cabinets, and in many kitchens the countertops and cabinets never move.

Signs to Look For

  • Opening the sink cabinet releases a stale, damp odor
  • Behind the dishwasher or sink, a wall with staining or blistered paint
  • A brown stain on the ceiling of the room below the kitchen
  • Doors on the cabinets that have gone out of alignment or started sticking
  • An unexplained jump in water use, or dripping you can hear when nothing is running
  • Swelling and flaking where the cabinet sides meet the floor
Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Under the Base Cabinets

Base cabinets sit over a gap that is boxed in all around. Water pools in it for weeks, and the mold that grows goes unseen until the cabinets come out.

Behind the Dishwasher

A slow drip at the supply or drain connection wets the subfloor and the cabinet sides next to it. Until the floor changes, the dishwasher covers everything.

The Ice Maker Line

The thin tube feeding the ice maker can leak a few drops a minute. Since the refrigerator is never pulled out, even that much rots the floor and the wall in back of it.

Layers of Flooring

Kitchens often have new flooring laid over old. Between those layers water is trapped, unable to evaporate and invisible from above.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Stop the Source

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Map the Moisture

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check cabinet bases, the wall behind them, and the flooring across the room. Under kitchen flooring, water often goes farther than anyone expects.

3

Dry in Place

We remove the toe kicks and direct air behind and beneath the cabinets. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

We carefully remove any flooring, subfloor or cabinet section that cannot be saved. After dry readings are confirmed, the appliances go back in and we help you arrange the remaining repairs.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First hour

Water Runs Under

When a hose or fitting fails, water heads behind the cabinets and beneath the appliances. Seams and edges let it get below the flooring.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Start Swelling

The water is absorbed by particleboard bases and toe kicks, which swell. In the dark, enclosed gap below the cabinets, and at the back of the dishwasher, mold can start.

Days 3 through 7

Flooring Starts to Move

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. Cabinet doors and drawers drift out of line.

After a week

Removal Becomes Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. Mold at the back of the cabinets often forces their removal, countertop included, and the subfloor may be delaminating.
